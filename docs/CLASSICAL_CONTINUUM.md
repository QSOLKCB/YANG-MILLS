# Phase 1E — classical continuum bundle/connection dictionary

## Scope

Phase 1E freezes the classical differential-geometric dictionary that the finite-lattice theory is intended to approximate. It connects the already-frozen `SU(2)`, `SU(3)`, center, and path-holonomy surfaces to standard principal-bundle language without changing their established discrete conventions.

The authoritative machine-readable surface is `YM-CLASSICAL-GEOMETRY-V1`.

This phase is **classical**. It does not construct the interacting quantum Yang–Mills theory required by the Clay problem, prove a positive mass gap, perform a continuum limit, or promote finite-lattice evidence to a theorem.

## Base manifold, tangent bundle, and cotangent bundle

Let `M` be a smooth manifold. At each point `x in M`, the tangent space is `T_x M` and the cotangent space is its dual `T_x^* M`.

The tangent and cotangent bundles are

```text
TM   = disjoint_union_x T_x M
T^*M = disjoint_union_x T_x^* M.
```

These are bundles built from the geometry of the **base manifold**. They are not gauge fibers.

When the four-dimensional Hodge-star and Yang–Mills functional conventions are invoked, Phase 1E assumes an oriented Riemannian four-manifold `(M,g)` with positive-definite metric. This is the Euclidean-signature setting relevant to the repository's current classical dictionary.

## Differential forms

A scalar differential `k`-form is an alternating `k`-linear covariant tensor field. In particular, a 2-form `alpha` assigns to each point `x` an alternating bilinear map

```text
alpha_x : T_x M x T_x M -> R.
```

For coefficient bundles `W`, write `Omega^k(M,W)` for `W`-valued `k`-forms when the coefficient bundle and induced connection have been specified.

The wedge product is graded. For scalar forms `alpha` of degree `p` and `beta` of degree `q`,

```text
alpha wedge beta = (-1)^(p q) beta wedge alpha.
```

When forms are matrix- or Lie-algebra-valued, the coefficient multiplication/bracket must also be included; the matrix product is not commutative even when the form degree gives a positive graded sign.

## Principal bundle

The gauge field is fundamentally described by a connection on a principal bundle

```text
pi : P -> M
```

with compact Lie group `G` acting freely on the **right**:

```text
R_g(p) = p g.
```

A principal connection may be represented by a Lie-algebra-valued connection one-form

```text
omega_P in Omega^1(P, g)
```

satisfying the standard reproduction and equivariance conditions

```text
omega_P(xi_sharp) = xi
R_g^* omega_P = Ad_(g^-1) omega_P.
```

Here `xi_sharp` is the fundamental vertical vector field generated by `xi in g`.

The principal fiber is not a tangent space of `M`, and the principal connection is not a section of an associated matter bundle.

## Associated vector bundle

Given a representation

```text
rho : G -> GL(V),
```

the associated vector bundle is

```text
E = P x_rho V
```

with equivalence convention

```text
(p,v) ~ (p g, rho(g^-1) v).
```

A section is a map

```text
s : M -> E
```

with

```text
pi_E o s = id_M.
```

Matter fields, representation-valued fields, and the adjoint bundle are expressed through sections of associated bundles. The repository currently studies pure Yang–Mills; introducing this general associated-bundle language does not add dynamical matter to the physical model.

## Local connection forms and the Phase 1D gauge convention

Choose a local section

```text
sigma : U -> P.
```

The local gauge potential is

```text
A = sigma^* omega_P in Omega^1(U,g).
```

It is a local representative of the principal connection. On a nontrivial bundle it must not be mistaken for one globally defined ordinary Lie-algebra-valued 1-form on all of `M`.

If the local section is changed by

```text
sigma' = sigma g,
```

then

```text
A' = g^-1 A g + g^-1 d g.
```

Phase 1D already froze a gauge variable called `Omega`. Phase 1E identifies

```text
Omega = g^-1.
```

Therefore the same transformation becomes exactly the frozen Phase 1D rule

```text
A^Omega
  = Omega A Omega^-1
    - dOmega Omega^-1.
```

This reconciliation is part of the Phase 1E contract. The principal-bundle convention and the already-frozen path convention are not independent sign choices.

## Associated local fields and covariant derivative

Under the same local section change, local associated-field coordinates transform as

```text
psi^Omega = rho(Omega) psi.
```

The principal connection induces

```text
nabla_A : Gamma(E)
        -> Gamma(T^*M tensor E).
```

Locally, in a matrix representation,

```text
D_A = d + rho_*(A).
```

Its Leibniz rule is

```text
nabla_A(f s)
  = d f tensor s
    + f nabla_A s.
```

The frozen gauge rule is

```text
D_(A^Omega)(rho(Omega) psi)
  = rho(Omega) D_A psi.
```

## `SU(3)` Lie-algebra bridge

Mathematically,

```text
su(3)
```

is the Lie algebra of traceless anti-Hermitian `3 x 3` matrices.

Phase 1B deliberately uses the canonical **Hermitian** generators

```text
T^a = lambda_a / 2.
```

These are not themselves anti-Hermitian Lie-algebra elements. The corresponding anti-Hermitian basis is

```text
i T^a.
```

Thus a real-coefficient local connection in the Phase 1B fundamental representation may be written

```text
A = i A_mu^a T^a dx^mu.
```

This bridge prevents the Phase 1B Hermitian generator convention from being silently confused with the mathematical definition of `su(3)`.

## Curvature

Globally, the Yang–Mills curvature is an adjoint-bundle-valued 2-form

```text
F_A in Omega^2(M, ad P).
```

In a local matrix representation,

```text
F_A = dA + A wedge A.
```

When the coefficient product is instead written using the Lie bracket, the same convention is

```text
F_A = dA + (1/2) [A wedge A].
```

Under a local gauge transformation,

```text
F_(A^Omega)
  = Omega F_A Omega^-1.
```

The Bianchi identity is

```text
D_A F_A = 0.
```

Gauge curvature is not any of the following:

```text
Riemann curvature of the base manifold
Gaussian curvature of an embedded surface
Frenet curvature of a path
path torsion
osculating-circle / osculating-plane geometry.
```

Those are different geometric objects with different bundles and definitions.

## Levi-Civita firewall

A Riemannian metric determines the Levi-Civita connection

```text
nabla^LC
```

on `TM`: the unique torsion-free, metric-compatible connection on the tangent bundle.

The gauge connection acts on the principal gauge bundle and its associated bundles. Therefore

```text
gauge connection != Levi-Civita connection
gauge curvature F_A != Riemann curvature R^LC.
```

Likewise, gauge parallel transport of associated-fiber data is not Levi-Civita parallel transport of tangent vectors.

## Riemannian metric, Hodge star, and fiber metric

An orientation and Riemannian metric on `M` define the Hodge star. In four Euclidean dimensions,

```text
* : Omega^k(M,W) -> Omega^(4-k)(M,W).
```

For 2-forms,

```text
*^2 = +1.
```

The Yang–Mills norm also requires an `Ad`-invariant positive-definite inner product on the compact Lie algebra. That fiber inner product is additional structure and is not the same object as the Riemannian metric on `TM`.

For a complex associated bundle with unitary representation, a Hermitian fiber metric may likewise be preserved by the induced connection. It remains distinct from the base-manifold Riemannian metric.

### Frozen 4D sign fixture

The executable Phase 1E regression uses oriented Euclidean `R^4` with orthonormal coframe `(e0,e1,e2,e3)` and positive volume form

```text
e0 wedge e1 wedge e2 wedge e3.
```

For the ordered 2-form basis

```text
01, 02, 03, 12, 13, 23
```

the Hodge star is frozen as

```text
*01 =  23
*02 = -13
*03 =  12
*12 =  03
*13 = -02
*23 =  01.
```

The Rust reference only checks these finite-dimensional sign conventions. It is not a numerical continuum-manifold solver.

## Formal adjoint and Yang–Mills equation

For the four-dimensional 2-form convention relevant to `F_A`, Phase 1E freezes

```text
D_A^* F_A = - * D_A * F_A.
```

The classical Yang–Mills functional is normalized as

```text
YM(A)
  = (1/2) integral_M |F_A|^2 dvol.
```

The norm uses both the base Riemannian metric and the chosen `Ad`-invariant Lie-algebra inner product.

Varying the connection gives the Euler–Lagrange equation

```text
D_A^* F_A = 0.
```

A **Yang–Mills connection** is therefore a critical point of `YM(A)`. A critical point is not automatically a global minimizer.

## Self-duality and anti-self-duality

Because `*^2=+1` on 2-forms in oriented Riemannian four dimensions, every 2-form decomposes as

```text
F_A = F_A^+ + F_A^-

F_A^+ = (F_A + *F_A)/2
F_A^- = (F_A - *F_A)/2.
```

These satisfy

```text
*F_A^+ = +F_A^+
*F_A^- = -F_A^-.
```

If

```text
F_A = +/- *F_A
```

and the Bianchi identity holds, then

```text
D_A^* F_A
  = - * D_A *F_A
  = 0.
```

Thus self-dual and anti-self-dual connections satisfy the classical Yang–Mills equation.

This classical implication has **no direct mass-gap implication**.

## Flat connections and global holonomy

A connection is flat when

```text
F_A = 0.
```

On a sufficiently small simply connected trivializing neighborhood, a flat connection is locally pure gauge. Globally, however, a flat connection can still have nontrivial holonomy when the topology/fundamental group permits it.

Therefore

```text
F_A = 0
```

does not by itself imply globally trivial holonomy.

## Parallel transport and reconciliation with Phase 1D

For an associated vector bundle, let

```text
P_gamma
```

denote the **forward** transport map from the fiber over `gamma(0)` to the fiber over `gamma(1)`.

With local field coordinates transforming as `psi^Omega=rho(Omega)psi`, forward transport transforms as

```text
P_gamma^Omega
  = rho(Omega(end))
    P_gamma
    rho(Omega(start))^-1.
```

Phase 1D already froze a start-left transporter `U_gamma` satisfying

```text
U_gamma^Omega
  = rho(Omega(start))
    U_gamma
    rho(Omega(end))^-1.
```

Phase 1E therefore freezes the dictionary

```text
U_gamma = P_gamma^-1.
```

This is why the Phase 1D evolution/product ordering looks opposite to some forward-transport textbook formulas while remaining mathematically consistent.

### Multiple trivializations

Phase 1D intentionally required a single chart containing the path image for its local formula. Phase 1E records the global rule: if a path crosses several trivializing neighborhoods, subdivide it into chart-contained segments and convert local fiber coordinates at chart boundaries using the associated transition functions.

If local sections satisfy

```text
sigma_j = sigma_i g_ij
```

on an overlap, then associated coordinates satisfy

```text
v_j = rho(g_ij^-1) v_i.
```

Local segment transport plus these transition-function coordinate changes represents one global parallel-transport map. No single local potential `A_i` is silently extended outside its chart.

## Lattice-to-continuum dictionary

Phase 1E freezes the following relationship without upgrading approximation to equality.

### Link

A lattice link

```text
U_mu(x)
```

approximates the frozen Phase 1D **start-left** transporter along one oriented lattice edge. The associated forward transport is its inverse.

### Plaquette

The oriented plaquette product is a finite small-loop holonomy. In a smooth small-spacing regime its expansion contains the continuum curvature component `F_mu_nu`.

The repository does **not** assert

```text
finite plaquette = pointwise F_mu_nu.
```

### Wilson loop

The normalized trace of discrete closed holonomy approximates the corresponding continuum holonomy observable only under a specified continuum limiting procedure.

### Wilson action

The finite-spacing Wilson action is a lattice discretization of the classical continuum Yang–Mills functional. Phase 1E does not freeze a group-specific scale-setting/renormalization formula and does not claim exact equality at nonzero lattice spacing.

No finite-lattice result is promoted to a continuum quantum theorem by this dictionary.

## Topological branch boundary

Instantons, characteristic classes, second Chern numbers, and Donaldson-theory connections are mathematically relevant to four-dimensional gauge theory, but they are not automatically evidence for the quantum mass gap.

Phase 1E records them as a possible later classical/topological branch. They require a separate contract if activated as a research surface.

## Claim boundary

Phase 1E establishes a convention-consistent classical differential-geometric dictionary plus finite-dimensional Hodge/self-duality regression checks.

It does **not** establish:

- an interacting quantum Yang–Mills construction;
- Osterwalder–Schrader/Wightman reconstruction;
- an infinite-volume or continuum limit of the lattice model;
- a positive spectral gap;
- confinement;
- the Clay Millennium theorem.
